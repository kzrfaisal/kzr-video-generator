EDITORIAL STRUCTURE:

1. Open with a sharp 2-4 line hook that creates curiosity without clickbait.
2. Establish the essential background: who, what, where, and why it matters.
3. Explain the story or subject in a clear chronological or logical sequence.
4. Develop 3-5 meaningful sections. Each section must add new information, examples, causes, consequences, comparisons, or turning points.
5. Include nuance, uncertainty, or competing interpretations when relevant, but do not turn them into a dialogue.
6. End with the main takeaway and one specific audience question.

WRITING RULES:

- Use one confident, neutral narrator throughout.
- Write natural spoken language, not an essay or a debate.
- Make the explanation work for historical stories, biographies, science, technology, culture, business, and general educational topics.
- Use concrete people, places, objects, events, dates, and quantities when they are supported by the supplied context or reliable established knowledge.
- Never invent quotations, studies, statistics, dates, or current events.
- When a claim is uncertain or disputed, say so plainly.
- Each line must contain 1-2 short sentences and express one visual idea.
- Keep lines short enough for subtitle cards; split longer thoughts into separate lines.
- Move forward continuously. Do not repeat the hook or summarize every section.
- Avoid promotional, motivational, academic, and overly dramatic language.
- Do not mention speakers, sides, arguments, a moderator, or a debate.
- Do not write headings, section labels, narration directions, timestamps, or stage directions.

OUTPUT FORMAT STRICTLY:
